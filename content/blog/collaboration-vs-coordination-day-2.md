Hello there! 👋

Picture two teams.

Team A has five developers. Each one picks up a task. By noon, five tasks are in progress. The board looks great. Everyone's busy.

Team B has five developers. They swarm on one task together. By noon, one task is done. The board looks... slow?

Now fast forward to Friday.

Team A still has five tasks in progress. Some are blocked. Some need review. Some are waiting on questions that haven't been answered yet. Maybe one or two actually ship.

Team B? They finished five tasks. One at a time. Each one fully done before starting the next.

Here's the thing we get wrong about productivity:

**Work in progress is not work completed.**

Starting is easy. Finishing is hard.

And every task you start but don't finish carries weight. Context you have to hold. Decisions waiting to be made. Merge conflicts growing in the background.

Team A optimized for looking busy.

Team B optimized for finishing.

The customer only pays for finished.

So the next time you're tempted to "maximize throughput" by spreading the team across tasks, ask yourself:

Are we starting more? Or finishing more?

Yours,
