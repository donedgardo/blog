Hello there! 👋

Let's talk about incentives.

Imagine two scenarios:

**Scenario A:** You bill by the hour. A gnarly bug lands on your desk. You struggle with it alone for eight hours. Finally fix it. Invoice: 8 hours.

**Scenario B:** Same bug. You grab a teammate. Together, you solve it in one hour. Invoice: 2 hours total.

Scenario B is better for everyone. Faster fix. Shared knowledge. Customer wins.

But Scenario A *pays more*.

And that's the trap.

When you bill time and materials, effort becomes the product. Hours become inventory. Struggling alone isn't just acceptable. It's profitable.

Collaboration? That feels like leaving money on the table. Two people, one hour, half the revenue.

No wonder teams resist it.

The incentives are backwards.

But here's what smart teams are figuring out: **bill for outcomes, not hours.**

When the customer pays for the solved problem, suddenly speed matters. Learning matters. Two heads finishing in one hour beats one head thrashing for eight.

Collaboration stops being a cost. It becomes the strategy.

The work doesn't change. The incentive does.

And incentives shape everything.
