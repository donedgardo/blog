Hello there! 👋

There's a phrase that's killed more progress than any technical debt:

*"It's working, why change it?"*

Six words. Perfectly reasonable on the surface. And absolutely lethal to improvement.

Here's the thing about teams that coordinate instead of collaborate: it works. Sort of.

Features ship. Eventually. Deadlines get met. Sometimes. The codebase grows. The team survives.

It's not broken. It's just... slow. Painful in ways that have become familiar.

And familiar pain is comfortable. We know where it hurts. We've built workarounds. We've made peace with the merge conflicts and the blocked tickets and the "quick sync" meetings that take an hour.

Unfamiliar improvement? That's scary.

Pair programming sounds exhausting. Swarming on one task sounds inefficient. Integrating every hour sounds excessive.

So we stick with what we know. We optimize around the pain instead of eliminating it.

*"It's working, why change it?"*

Because "working" isn't the same as "good."

Because familiar pain is still pain.

Because the team that tries something new might discover something better. And the team that doesn't, won't.

The biggest risk isn't changing what works.

It's never learning what's possible.

Yours,
Ed
