Hello there! 👋

Here's the insight that ties it all together:

**Coordination adds. Collaboration compounds.**

A coordinating team gets a little better each quarter. Someone learns something, writes it in a doc, maybe shares it at a retro. Knowledge spreads slowly, if it spreads at all.

A collaborating team gets better every day. Every session. Every hour.

When you solve a problem together, the learning is instant. What one person knows, everyone knows. The debugging trick. The cleaner pattern. The faster way.

That knowledge doesn't wait for a wiki page. It transfers in real time.

And here's the thing about compound growth: it doesn't feel dramatic at first. Day one, the teams look similar. Week one, maybe the same.

But give it a month. A quarter. A year.

The coordinating team has added knowledge. Slowly. Linearly.

The collaborating team has multiplied it. Each insight building on the last. Each person accelerating the others.

Same people. Same problems. Wildly different trajectories.

This is what's possible when you stop dividing the work and start sharing it.

Not just faster.

Exponentially better.

That's collaboration.
